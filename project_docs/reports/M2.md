# M2 — Alignment (1.4B → 410M, the primary width-only pair)

**Status: PASS.** Per-layer R² reported for both alignment variants; ridge wins; and we can now say *how much* apparent cross-width difference alignment removes — with an important activation-vs-weight contrast that sets up M4.

Primary pair: **1.4B (24 layers, d=2048) → 410M (24 layers, d=1024)** — same depth, so layer correspondence is 1:1 and the science isolates *width*. All measurements reuse the M1 caches (no new capture).

## 2a. Layer correspondence — linear CKA (`experiments/m2_cka.py`)

24×24 CKA on the CKA-subset activations (all token positions). `diag_mean = 0.883`, but only 12% of layers have their single best match exactly on the diagonal — because the heatmap is a **big saturated block**: layers 0–2 distinct, layers ~4–22 all mutually ≈1.0, layer 23 distinct.

**Read:** linear CKA **saturates** in the middle band (a known limitation), so it confirms *coarse* early/mid/late correspondence but is **not a sharp per-layer discriminator**. Motivates the R² maps below.

## 2b/2c. Width-conversion maps — ridge vs Procrustes (`experiments/m2_maps.py`)

Per layer, fit large→small on pooled activations (8k train / 2k held-out), score R²:

| Map | Freedom | Mean held-out R² |
|---|---|---|
| **ridge** | full linear 2048→1024 | **0.844** |
| **procrustes** | rotation + one global scale | **0.717** |

**Winner: ridge.** The 410M pooled representation is **largely a linear projection** of the 1.4B's (84% of held-out variance). A pure rotation+scale already reaches 72%, so most of the relationship is a change of basis; the **~0.13 gap** is genuine non-orthogonal reshaping. Discriminative per layer (both dip together at layers 3–5 — the same layers CKA flagged as distinct).

## 2.2. Weight-space alignment — embeddings (`experiments/m2_weights.py`)

The token embeddings are the one weight matrix where the two sizes share an axis (vocabulary) and differ only in width, so a single map applies. Rows (tokens) split 80/20.

| Matrix | CKA (before) | ridge R² | procrustes R² |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMB_IN  | 0.512 | 0.248 | 0.167 |
| EMB_OUT | 0.678 | 0.386 | 0.242 |

**The key contrast:** **activations align strongly (R²≈0.84) but the raw weights align weakly (R²≈0.25–0.39).** Two independently trained models arrive at closely related *representations* while their *parameters* are only loosely a linear map of each other — even in the cleanest case.

## How much does alignment remove?

- **Representation space:** a lot — from incomparable (different width/basis) to R²=0.84. Alignment removes most of the apparent difference.
- **Parameter space:** much less — even the best-case embeddings leave the majority of variance unexplained by a linear map.

That gap **is** the Paper-1 question: after we project weights (M3), the residual `W_small − Project(W_large)` will be substantial. **M4** asks whether that residual is *structured/learnable* or *noise*.
