# M5 — The recovery race (matched-budget fine-tuning of conversion inits)

**Verdict: transfer inits beat training from scratch at matched budget — and structure-respecting selection beats dense blending ~3×.** The practical claim of the project survives M4's null: the value of size conversion lives in *initialization*, exactly as M4 predicted.

Three arms, identical everything (data stream, order, AdamW + cosine schedule, 30M tokens, bf16, single seed): the only difference is the starting weights. All arms are obtainable **without** the trained 410M (no target leakage).

## Zero-shot starting line (quick-eval ppl, 50k wikitext tokens)

| Init | ppl before any training |
|---|---|
| subclone (selection) | 11,509 |
| projection (dense blend, **fresh LNs**) | 12,323 |
| random | ~66,000 |

**Correction to M3 (important):** M3's zero-shot number for the dense projection (181,242, "worse than random") used the real 410M's LayerNorms; the projected-LN variant exploded to 10¹³. With **neutral fresh LayerNorms (gain 1, bias 0)** the same projected matrices score ~12.3k — on par with subclone's starting line. So a large share of M3's zero-shot catastrophe was **LayerNorm mismatch**, not weight destruction alone. The structure-mixing diagnosis stands directionally (12k is still ~800× worse than the real model), but the "worse than random" phrasing was an LN artifact. A correcting note was added to M3.md.

## Reading

1. **Both transfer inits beat from-scratch** at matched compute — conversion has real initialization value. (The plan's practical question, answered positively.)
2. **Selection ≫ blend as an init** (356 vs 1,055): preserving valid wiring matters not just zero-shot but for *trainability* — the blend's scrambled structure costs it throughout training, supporting the M3 diagnosis.
3. All arms remain far from the real 410M (15.6) — expected: 30M tokens is ~0.01% of Pythia's 300B-token pretraining. The race measures *relative* recovery speed, not end quality.

## Caveats (honest list)

- **Single seed, single budget.** Gaps are large (3–4×), unlikely to be seed noise, but the subclone-vs-projection ordering should be confirmed with 2–3 seeds before a strong paper claim. 30M ≪ the plan's suggested 200M; curves had not crossed and were still separating at cutoff.
- t=0 points are quick-eval (50k tokens); final numbers are full-eval. Mixed on the curve figure (t=0 vs finals) — same eval within each series.
- Random-init curve uses the same LR schedule tuned for nothing in particular; a from-scratch-optimized schedule might narrow its gap somewhat.

## Compute accounting

- Training: identical 30M tokens/arm, ~55 min each on the GB10 (9.4k tok/s), ~2h50m total.
- Init construction costs (one-time, negligible vs training): subclone selection = norms + topk, seconds; projection = PCA on cached activations + per-layer SVDs, ~2 min; both amortize to ~0 against any training budget. No lm-eval tasks this round (deferred since M3; the assembled models are still far from task-capable).

## Runner 4 — hybrid: subclone + least-squares compensation (OURS; the winner)

Motivated by a design question — can selection's wiring be combined with projection-style re-fitting? — keep subclone's selection (which wires survive), then re-fit the surviving weights at the two purely-linear spots — MLP_DOWN (post-GELU hidden → residual) and O (attention context → residual) — by closed-form ridge least squares against input second moments measured on 1,000 frozen-corpus sequences (`second_moments` + `ls_compensate`). The survivors absorb the deleted wires' correlated contribution. Read-in weights are not compensated in v1 (the small LN renormalizes over kept features, making that target ill-defined).

| Milestone | Hybrid | Subclone (same point) |
|---|---|---|
| zero-shot | **9,664** | 11,509 |
| 10M tokens | **538** | 1,468 |
| ~14M tokens | **≈356 (matches subclone's FINAL)** | — |
| 30M final (full) | **114.1** | 355.8 |

**Verdict: the hybrid wins decisively — 3.1× better than subcloning at identical budget, and it reaches subcloning's final quality with <half the tokens.** Full standings: hybrid 114.1 < subclone 355.8 < projection 1,054.5 < random 1,519.0 (13.3× better than from-scratch). Compensation cost: one moments pass (~75 s) + closed-form solves (seconds) — negligible.

Positioning note (for the paper): least-squares reconstruction after pruning exists in the compression literature; the contribution here is the combination and setting — family *size conversion* (not same-model compression), rotary-frequency-matched selection, and the controlled M2–M5 characterization around it. Same single-seed caveat as the other arms.

### Ablation ladder & the rescale correction (R3.3 follow-through, 2026-07-31)

Testing the reference recipe's √(d/d′) rescale produced a **major correction to the headline**. Full ladder (pair A, 30M tokens, seed 0; seeds 1–2 for the top two arms in flight):

| Init | zero-shot ppl | final ppl |
|---|---|---|
| random | ~66,000 | 1,519.0 |
| projection (dense) | 12,323 | 1,054.5 |
| subclone, plain selection | 11,509 | 355.8 |
| + isotropic scale on O/MLP_DOWN only (`iso`) | 26,669 | 208.2 |
| + LS compensation (**hybrid**) | **9,664** | 114.1 |
| subclone + full √2 rescale (reference recipe, `rs`) | 61,912 | 86.4 |
| **hybrid + read-path rescale (`hybrid_rs`)** | 18,459 | **83.0** |

**Readings.**
1. **The reference rescale is a training-dynamics lever, not a function fix** — it *wrecks* zero-shot (61.9k) yet its endpoint (86.4) beats plain subcloning 4.1×. My earlier reasoning ("LayerNorm renormalizes, so rescale is unnecessary") was correct about *function* and wrong about *trainability* — the reference authors' recipe is vindicated, and the initial "3.1× over subcloning" headline was measured against the weaker (unrescaled) variant. Corrected here.
2. **Two independent levers, and they compose**: compensation = *function* lever (best zero-shot: 9.7k); rescale = *training* lever (best finals). Their combination `hybrid_rs` **dominates the strongest baseline on both axes** (zero-shot 18.5k vs 61.9k; final 83.0 vs 86.4).
3. Monotone trade-off along the ladder: more rescaling → worse start, better endpoint.
4. **Honest margins**: hybrid_rs's final edge over subclone_rs is 4% at 30M, single seed — seeds 1–2 for both arms are running; the claim will be stated with error bars (and dropped to "parity with better zero-shot" if the bars overlap).

Practical guidance (paper): *need a working model immediately → hybrid; will fine-tune → hybrid_rs.*

## Hardening (R3.1 + budget-persistence; run 2026-07-30)

### Beyond wikitext-1024: all metrics, same ordering

Zero-shot task accuracies (lm-eval, 0-shot) + out-of-domain and long-context ppl for the four 30M checkpoints and the real 410M reference. Guess floors: arc_easy 0.25, hellaswag 0.25, piqa 0.50, lambada ≈0 (open-vocabulary).

| Model | C4 ppl | wikitext ppl @2048 ctx | lambada acc | arc_easy | hellaswag | piqa |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **hybrid** | **90.7** | **109.8** | **0.076** | **0.313** | **0.266** | **0.555** |
| subclone | 162.7 | 346.3 | 0.005 | 0.308 | 0.263 | 0.548 |

| random | 518.8 | 1,788 | 0 | 0.264 | 0.256 | 0.532 |

**The ordering hybrid < subclone < projection < random holds on every metric.** Long-context is the standout: ppl at 2048 (2× the fine-tuning length) matches each model's 1024 number — the rotary-frequency-matched selection extrapolates cleanly. Lambada (floor ≈0) is the sharpest ability signal: hybrid 14× subclone. Multiple-choice tasks sit near their floors for all four checkpoints — 30M tokens buys language modeling, not task reasoning; we claim only the former. Real-410M scores match published Pythia numbers (eval pipeline sanity ✓).

### Unseen pair (410M→160M), 3 seeds — generality + error bars

A conversion regime the method was never tuned on: depth **halves** (24→12 blocks, evenly strided), heads 16→12 (whole-head selection; head_dim unchanged so rotary untouched), width cuts only ~25%. Seeds vary the training-data draw; identical budget/schedule (30M tokens; ~19k tok/s at this scale). Final full wikitext ppl:

| Init | s0 | s1 | s2 | mean ± std |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **hybrid** | 117.5 | 113.6 | 108.5 | **113.2 ± 4.5** |
| subclone | 115.0 | 121.6 | 118.9 | 118.5 ± 3.3 |
| random | 1,464.4 | 1,571.5 | 1,479.6 | 1,505.2 ± 57.9 |

**Verdicts.** (1) The core claim generalizes with error bars: transfer inits beat from-scratch **~13×** on a never-seen pair, every seed. (2) Hybrid vs subclone is a **statistical tie** here (overlapping ±1σ; hybrid nominally ahead) — unlike the width-dominated primary pair (3.1×). **Boundary insight:** compensation repairs *width* cuts (survivors absorb deleted wires); deleted *blocks* have no survivors to re-tune, and this pair's damage is depth-dominated. So compensation's edge scales with the width reduction — and costs ~nothing where it's neutral. Zero-shot starting lines echo this: hybrid ≈30k here (vs 9.7k on the width pair), random ≈66k. Reduction-aware conversion (block rescale/distill; width-vs-depth budget allocation) is planned extension work.

**100M spot-check (seed 0):** hybrid 77.1 vs subclone 74.4 — the tie persists at 3.3× the budget (the 3.6% gap is within the 30M seed noise, and the nominal leader flips between budgets). The width-vs-depth boundary is budget-robust, not an artifact of short training.

**Metric-robustness (seed-0 checkpoints + real 160M anchor):** the tie also holds on every other metric — C4 ppl 91.1 (hybrid) vs 94.2 (subclone), ctx-2048 111.8 vs 110.2, lambada 11.0% vs 10.7%, arc/hellaswag/piqa within noise of each other — while random sits at C4 514 / lambada 0. (Side note: these 160M-class checkpoints reach *higher* lambada than the 410M-class pair-A ones at the same budget — smaller models recover language skill faster.) Real-160M anchor (lambada 35.4%) matches published Pythia numbers.
