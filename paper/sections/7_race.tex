\section{Matched-budget conversion: two independent levers}
\label{sec:race}

The characterization so far is diagnostic: representations align
(\S\ref{sec:representations}), dense projection is functionally destructive
(\S\ref{sec:projection}), and its post-operator residuals are noise
(\S\ref{sec:residuals}). The constructive claim follows here. If conversion
value lives in \emph{initialization}, the decisive test is a matched-budget
recovery race: give every candidate the same target-size shape, the same data,
schedule, and token budget, and let continued pre-training reveal which
starting weights convert fastest. We find that the init decomposes into two
\emph{independent} levers --- least-squares \textbf{compensation}, which fixes
what the init \emph{computes} (best zero-shot), and variance-preserving
\textbf{rescale}, which fixes the \emph{scale the optimizer sees} (best
endpoints) --- and that stacking both dominates the strongest single-lever
variant on both axes and in every seed.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
All arms share the primary pair's target shape (410M), identical data order,
optimizer, cosine schedule, and token budget; only the starting weights differ
(full protocol in \S\ref{sec:setup}). Crucially, \emph{every} init is
obtainable without the trained target: selection scores, compensation moments,
and rescale factors are all read off the donor 1.4B and the frozen corpus, so
no arm leaks target weights. Zero-shot columns are quick-eval perplexity
(50k WikiText tokens) at $t{=}0$; final columns are full strided WikiText-103
perplexity at the 30M-token budget. Unless a $\pm$ appears, a cell is a single
seed (footnoted).

\paragraph{Both transfer inits beat from scratch.}
Even bare structured selection (\textsc{subclone}, 355.8) recovers $4.3\times$
faster than random (1{,}519.0) at matched compute, and dense projection
(1{,}054.5) --- despite its scrambled wiring --- still beats from scratch
$1.4\times$. Selection preserves valid wiring (rotary pairing, per-head
attention, elementwise GELU, per-feature LayerNorm), and that structure pays
off not merely at $t{=}0$ but throughout training: \textsc{subclone} separates
from \textsc{projection} almost immediately and the gap widens monotonically
(Fig.~\ref{fig:m5curves}). The full ladder closes at $1{,}519.0/83.0 =
18\times$ better than from scratch.

\subsection{Two independent levers}
\label{sec:race:levers}

Reading the ladder by \emph{construction} rather than by rank exposes two
orthogonal axes over the selection base.

\paragraph{Compensation is the function lever.}
The hybrid re-fits the two purely-linear, LayerNorm-free read-out paths --- the
attention output projection $O$ and the MLP down-projection --- by closed-form
ridge least squares (\S\ref{sec:race:alg}), so the surviving units absorb the
correlated contribution of the units selection dropped. This strictly improves
\emph{both} axes: zero-shot falls $11{,}509\to9{,}664$ (the best zero-shot in
the table) and the endpoint falls $355.8\to114.1$ ($3.1\times$ over
\textsc{subclone}). Compensation changes what the initial network computes, and
a better function is better everywhere.

\paragraph{Rescale is the training-dynamics lever.}
The reference recipe's variance-preserving factor
$\sqrt{d_{\text{in}}/d_{\text{in}}'}$ \citep{samragh2023weight} does the
opposite. Applied to the LayerNorm-free read-out paths only
(\textsc{subclone\_iso}) it \emph{worsens} zero-shot $11{,}509\to26{,}669$ yet
\emph{improves} the endpoint $355.8\to208.2$; applied to every input-cut path
(\textsc{subclone\_rs}) it collapses zero-shot to $61{,}912$ --- $5.4\times$
worse than bare selection --- while driving the endpoint down to $86.4$, past
even the compensated hybrid. Rescale does not fix the function; a downstream
LayerNorm renormalizes each rescaled read path, so the scalar cancels for the
forward pass and merely perturbs the delicate zero-shot alignment. What it
does fix is the variance the optimizer starts from, and along the rescale axis
the trade is monotone: more rescale, worse start, better endpoint.

\paragraph{The levers stack.}
Because the two levers act on disjoint quantities --- compensation on the
function of the two LN-free read-out paths, rescale on the scale of the
LN-fronted read-in paths ($Q,K,V$, the MLP up-projection, and the output
embedding) that compensation deliberately leaves alone --- they compose.
\textsc{hybrid\_rs} applies compensation where it is the ridge-optimal linear
map and the reference-recipe scale where compensation cannot reach, and
dominates the strongest single-lever arm (\textsc{subclone\_rs}) on
\emph{both} axes: zero-shot $18{,}459 < 61{,}912$ (compensation offsets the
rescale collapse) and endpoint $83.0 < 86.4$.

\paragraph{An honest correction.}
The rescale lever is a correction to our own earlier reading. When the hybrid
first won at $114.1$ we argued that its least-squares compensation
\emph{subsumed} the reference recipe's scalar rescale, on the grounds that a
ridge-optimal linear map is strictly better than a scalar on the paths it
re-fits. The upset --- \textsc{subclone\_rs} at $86.4$, beating the hybrid with
no compensation at all --- showed that argument is only half right. Compensation
does dominate rescale \emph{on the two read-out paths it touches}; but the
LN-fronted read-in paths, which compensation leaves untouched because the
LayerNorm makes their reconstruction target ill-defined, still benefit from
the reference recipe's variance correction as a training-dynamics prior. Blanket
rescale collapses zero-shot (it mis-scales reads a LayerNorm would have
renormalized), yet it wins on dynamics --- so the reference recipe is
vindicated exactly where our first account had written it off, and the
principled method is the stack, not either lever alone.

\subsection{Compensated Selection}
\label{sec:race:alg}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:compsel} states the full construction. Selection is the
architecture-aware step: it keeps whole coordinates so the symmetry structure of
each subspace survives. Residual features are chosen once, globally, by
activation variance (a shared stream admits one basis). Attention keeps
frequency-matched rotary pairs --- with rotary half-width $16\to8$ the small
frequency ladder is exactly every other large frequency, so we keep pairs at
stride $2$ and preserve the $(\cos,\sin)$ pairing \citep{su2024roformer} --- and
tops up the non-rotary dims by $\lVert q\rVert+\lVert k\rVert$ row norm; the
value/output path keeps the top head dims by $\lVert V_{\text{row}}\rVert\cdot
\lVert O_{\text{col}}\rVert$; MLP units by $\lVert\text{up}_{\text{row}}\rVert
\cdot\lVert\text{down}_{\text{col}}\rVert$. When the head \emph{count} shrinks
(the held-out pair) selection is promoted to whole heads scored by
$\sum_d\lVert V_{\text{row},d}\rVert\lVert O_{\text{col},d}\rVert$, one keep-set
shared by $Q,K,V,O$; when depth shrinks, evenly strided blocks are kept and each
kept block's norms and biases ride along.

Compensation is the closed-form optimum for a pruned linear map. For a map
$M$ reading a signal with measured second moment $\Sigma$, the ridge
least-squares substitute on the kept columns $\mathcal{K}$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lscomp}
M' \;=\; \arg\min_{M'} \; \mathbb{E}\,\big\lVert Mx - M'x_{\mathcal{K}}\big\rVert^2
\;=\; M\,\Sigma_{:,\mathcal{K}}\,\big(\Sigma_{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}} + \lambda I\big)^{-1},
\end{equation}
with plain subcloning as the special case $M'=M_{:,\mathcal{K}}$. We apply it at
the two spots where the reconstruction target is well-defined --- $O$ and the MLP
down-projection, both purely linear with no normalization between the cut and the
read --- and leave the LN-fronted read-in paths to the optional rescale lever.
Least-squares reconstruction after pruning is classical
\citep{hassibi1993optimal,ma2023llmpruner}; the contribution here is the setting
(family \emph{size conversion}, not same-model compression), the
rotary-frequency-matched selection, and the two-lever decomposition around it.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Compensated Selection (\textsc{hybrid\_rs})}
\label{alg:compsel}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE donor weights $W_L$; frozen-corpus activation variances and second
moments $\Sigma$; target shape (residual $d'$, head dim, MLP width, block map)
\ENSURE installable small-model weights (no target-model access)
\STATE $\mathcal{R} \gets$ top-$d'$ residual features by mean-over-layer activation variance \COMMENT{one global basis}
\FOR{each kept block $b$}
  \STATE $\mathcal{K}_{qk} \gets$ frequency-matched rotary pairs (stride $2$) $\cup$ top non-rotary dims by $\lVert q\rVert{+}\lVert k\rVert$
  \STATE $\mathcal{K}_{vo} \gets$ top head dims by $\lVert V_{\text{row}}\rVert\cdot\lVert O_{\text{col}}\rVert$ \COMMENT{whole heads if head count shrinks}
  \STATE $\mathcal{K}_{mlp} \gets$ top units by $\lVert\text{up}_{\text{row}}\rVert\cdot\lVert\text{down}_{\text{col}}\rVert$
  \STATE slice $W_L$ block $b$ to $(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{K}_{qk},\mathcal{K}_{vo},\mathcal{K}_{mlp})$; LayerNorms and biases ride along
  \STATE $O' \gets \text{LScomp}(O,\,\Sigma_{\text{attn-in}},\,\mathcal{K}_{vo})$;\; $\text{MLP\_down}' \gets \text{LScomp}(\text{MLP\_down},\,\Sigma_{\text{mlp-in}},\,\mathcal{K}_{mlp})$ \COMMENT{Eq.~\ref{eq:lscomp}, function lever}
  \IF{rescale}
    \STATE multiply LN-fronted read-in paths $Q,K,V,\text{MLP\_up}$ (and output embedding) by $\sqrt{d_{\text{in}}/d_{\text{in}}'}$ \COMMENT{dynamics lever}
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\RETURN assembled small model
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Hardening}
\label{sec:race:hardening}

The headline is one metric at one budget on one pair. We stress it on all three.

\paragraph{Every metric, same ordering.}
Table~\ref{tab:extended} adds out-of-domain perplexity (C4), perplexity at
$2\times$ the training context (2048), and four zero-shot tasks for the four
original inits and the real-410M anchor. The ordering
$\text{hybrid} < \text{subclone} < \text{projection} < \text{random}$ holds on
every metric. Long context is the cleanest signal: perplexity at 2048 tracks
each model's 1024 number, so the rotary-frequency-matched selection extrapolates
past its training length without penalty. LAMBADA (floor $\approx0$) is the
sharpest ability gap --- hybrid $14\times$ subclone --- while the multiple-choice
tasks sit near their guess floors for all four 30M checkpoints: 30M tokens buys
language modeling, not task reasoning, and we claim only the former. Anchor
scores match published Pythia numbers.

\paragraph{The lead persists at longer budget.}
Re-running the two leaders at 100M tokens, hybrid finishes at $62.3$ and
subclone at $103.1$; hybrid crosses subclone's \emph{100M} endpoint at
$\sim$38M tokens, a $\sim$$2.6\times$ token-efficiency gain.\footnote{100M cells
are single-seed. Cross-horizon comparisons are schedule-confounded: the cosine
schedule stretches with the budget, so we compare finals only \emph{within} a
horizon (e.g.\ we do not read hybrid-30M's $114.1$ against subclone-100M's
$103.1$ as a controlled result).}

\paragraph{A held-out pair, and the method's boundary.}
The 410M$\to$160M pair exercises axes the primary pair does not: depth
\emph{halves} ($24\to12$ blocks), the head count drops ($16\to12$, whole-head
selection), and width cuts are mild ($\sim$25\%). Across three seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:unseen}), transfer beats from scratch $\sim$$13\times$ on every
seed --- the core claim generalizes with error bars to a regime the method was
never tuned on. But here hybrid and subclone are a statistical \emph{tie}
(overlapping $\pm1\sigma$; hybrid nominally ahead), unlike the $3.1\times$
compensation gap on the width-dominated primary pair. The boundary is
mechanistic: compensation repairs \emph{width} cuts because surviving units can
absorb dropped ones, but deleted \emph{blocks} have no survivors to re-tune, and
this pair's damage is depth-dominated. Compensation's edge therefore scales with
the width reduction --- and costs essentially nothing where it is neutral. The
zero-shot starting lines echo this: hybrid starts at $\approx$30k here versus
$9.7$k on the width pair. The tie is budget-robust: at 100M tokens (seed 0)
hybrid $77.1$ vs.\ subclone $74.4$, a 3.6\% gap inside the seed noise with the
nominal leader flipped --- not an artifact of short training. It also holds on
every other metric (C4 $91.1$ vs.\ $94.2$, ctx-2048 $111.8$ vs.\ $110.2$,
LAMBADA $11.0\%$ vs.\ $10.7\%$, others within noise), while random sits at C4
$514$ and LAMBADA $0$; the real-160M anchor (LAMBADA $35.4\%$) matches published
Pythia numbers.\footnote{The 100M and seed-0 metric-robustness cells are
single-seed. Width-vs-depth budget allocation and reduction-aware conversion
(block distillation) are future work (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).}

\paragraph{Compute accounting.}
Initialization construction is negligible against any training budget and is
included in the accounting. Selection is norms plus top-$k$ (seconds); the
compensation moments pass over 1{,}000 frozen-corpus sequences is $\sim$75\,s;
the closed-form solves of Eq.~\ref{eq:lscomp} are seconds; the rescale lever is
a scalar multiply (free). Every arm then trains on the \emph{same} 30M tokens
under the \emph{same} schedule ($\sim$55\,min each on a single GB10 at
$\sim$9.4k tok/s), so all standings in Tables~\ref{tab:ladder}--\ref{tab:seeded}
compare equal-budget endpoints whose only difference is the starting weights.
