\section{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{One family, one wiring.}
All experiments are within the Pythia suite (GPT-NeoX blocks: LayerNorm,
elementwise GELU, full multi-head attention with rotary embeddings). This is
deliberate --- identical data and tokenizer isolate size --- but the two-lever
decomposition leans on exactly those wiring details: which read paths a
normalization fronts determines where compensation is well-posed and where
rescale acts (\S\ref{sec:race:alg}). Llama- and Qwen-class models change the
load-bearing parts --- RMSNorm in place of LayerNorm, gated SwiGLU MLPs whose
hidden units couple \emph{two} input matrices, grouped-query attention that
ties key/value heads across query heads --- so porting the method requires a
per-architecture safe-wiring analysis (which units may be selected together;
which paths a norm protects), not a mechanical re-run. The characterization
tools themselves (alignment maps, shuffle controls, the matched-budget race)
are architecture-agnostic.

\paragraph{Budgets far from convergence.}
Our recovery budgets (30M tokens primary, 100M persistence) are $\sim$0.01\%
of Pythia's 300B-token pretraining, and every converted model remains far
from the real target's quality (best endpoint $83.0$ vs.\ the real 410M's
$15.6$). All claims are therefore about \emph{relative recovery speed at
matched budget}, not end quality; whether the orderings persist to full
recovery is untested.

\paragraph{Seed counts.}
The decisive comparisons carry three data-draw seeds each (the seeded verdict
of Table~\ref{tab:seeded}; the held-out pair of Table~\ref{tab:unseen}); the
remaining ladder rungs, the 100M persistence checks, and the scale pair
(\S\ref{sec:race:scale}) are single-seed. We report $\pm$ only where seeds
exist and footnote every single-seed cell.

\paragraph{Read-in compensation is unexplored.}
Compensation is applied only where the reconstruction target is well-defined:
the two LayerNorm-free read-out paths. On the LN-fronted read-in paths, the
same renormalization that makes blanket rescale collapse zero-shot
(\S\ref{sec:race:levers}) makes the least-squares target ill-defined --- the
norm re-standardizes whatever the cut changes, so there is no fixed signal to
reconstruct. Whether a normalization-aware compensation exists for these
paths is open, and it is the most direct route to further improving the
zero-shot axis.

\paragraph{Depth is the uncompensable axis.}
The held-out pair locates the method's boundary
(\S\ref{sec:race:hardening}): compensation repairs width cuts because
surviving units can absorb dropped ones, but a deleted block leaves no
survivors to re-tune. Two directions follow naturally, and we have attempted
neither: reduction-aware conversion that recovers deleted blocks' function
into surviving neighbors (a distillation-flavored step), and choosing the
width-versus-depth reduction mix for a given parameter budget --- our
measured boundary suggests preferring width where the budget allows. We would
welcome follow-up on both.
