\section{Why dense projection fails}
\label{sec:projection}

If representations align across sizes, the natural attempt is to project the
large model's weights into the small model's shape and read off a working small
model. We formulate this projection using only the size relationship (never the
target's weights), measure the residual, run the assembled model, and diagnose
why it breaks. The verdict is that dense blending destroys the network's
structured computation --- and, via a bit-exact control, that the destruction is
in the projection mathematics, not the assembly.

\paragraph{Target-free projection.}
Each weight is mapped by a pair of structured linear operators,
\begin{equation}
\hat{W} \;=\; P_{\text{out}}\, W\, P_{\text{in}}^{\top},
\qquad
P_{\bullet} \in \{\, P_{\text{res}},\, P_{QK},\, P_{VO},\, P_{\text{MLP}} \,\},
\label{eq:project}
\end{equation}
whose bases are built \emph{only} from the large model and the size
relationship, never from the 410M target. The residual-stream axis uses a global
activation-PCA basis $P_{\text{res}}$; the internal axes use SVD bases
\emph{shared between interacting weights}, so that composed operations stay
consistent --- $Q$ and $K$ share one basis, $V$ and $O$ share one, and
\texttt{MLP\_UP}/\texttt{MLP\_DOWN} share a single joint basis computed from the
column stack $[\mathrm{UP},\,\mathrm{DOWN}^{\top}]$. Sharing is not cosmetic: a
per-weight basis for the MLP hidden axis cannot span the target, since one
$8192\times2048$ matrix has rank $\le 2048 < 4096$, so the interacting pair must
be factorized together.

\paragraph{The projected weights miss the target solution.}
Table~\ref{tab:proj} reports the relative Frobenius error of $\hat{W}$ against
the actual 410M weights, averaged over 24 layers, for the target-free projection
of Eq.~\ref{eq:project} and --- as a diagnostic upper bound --- a shared operator
$(A,B)$ \emph{fitted} with access to the target. Every target-free error exceeds
$1$, i.e.\ worse than predicting zero: the projected weights share almost nothing
with the 410M's particular solution, confirming the activation-versus-weight gap
of \S\ref{sec:representations} at every layer. Even the fitted operator, which
peeks at the target, only reaches $0.66$--$0.80$; at best $\sim$30\% of weight
variance is linearly explainable across sizes with one shared map.

\paragraph{Behavioral anchors.}
We assemble a 410M-shaped model from the projected 1.4B weights and score
strided WikiText-103 perplexity against reference and anchor models
(Table~\ref{tab:anchor}). Greedy generation is deterministic but word salad, and
the acceptance bar --- beat random init --- is not met zero-shot. The
\emph{shape} of the failure is instructive: random init produces near-uniform
logits (perplexity on the order of the vocabulary size), whereas the projected
model is confidently wrong, which scores far worse than uniform.

\paragraph{The failure is projection math, not assembly.}
Before diagnosing the mechanism, we rule out a plumbing bug. Rebuilding the 1.4B
from its own extracted weights with \emph{no} projection reproduces the real
model's logits with $\max|\text{diff}| = 0$ --- bit-exact (previewed in
\S\ref{sec:setup}). Extraction, head-aware QKV split/fuse, embeddings, and
LayerNorm plumbing are all correct, so the entire quality loss in
Table~\ref{tab:anchor} is attributable to Eq.~\ref{eq:project}.

\paragraph{Structure mixing breaks the computation.}
A dense basis change on any internal axis silently changes what the block
\emph{computes}, because every structured or nonlinear operation is tied to
specific coordinates. \emph{Rotary embeddings} act on fixed per-head dimension
pairs, so any head-space mixing distorts positions. \emph{Attention is per-head},
yet our global $2048\!\to\!1024$ head-space basis mixes dimensions \emph{across}
heads. \emph{GELU is elementwise} in the MLP hidden space, so
$\mathrm{GELU}(Bx)\neq B\,\mathrm{GELU}(x)$. \emph{LayerNorm gains and biases are
per-feature} in the residual stream, so a rotated stream no longer matches any
diagonal gain --- which is why the projected-LN variant explodes to $10^{13}$.
The consequence is that function-preserving zero-shot conversion needs
structure-respecting \emph{selection} maps --- keep or drop whole heads, hidden
units, and features, the architecture's own symmetry group --- rather than dense
blending. Dense learned projections are thus useful as \emph{initializations} for
brief fine-tuning, not as drop-in models; \S\ref{sec:race} takes up exactly that
comparison.
